# Email Security & Incident Reporting Policy

**Policy code:** POL-EML-001 · **Version:** 1.0 · **Applies to:** All staff
**Policy owner:** Administrator · **Approved by:** Savikro Enterprises Management

## 1. Purpose

Email is the most common way criminals attack businesses like Savikro. A single convincing email pretending to be from a supplier, customer or colleague can lead to a payment sent to the wrong bank account, stolen passwords or infected computers. This policy explains how to recognise suspicious messages, what to do about them, and how to report any security concern quickly.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all staff and covers company email, SMS, WhatsApp and other messaging, and phone calls related to Savikro business.

## 3. Warning signs of a suspicious message

Be suspicious of any message, even one that appears to come from someone you know, that shows one or more of these signs:

1. **Urgency or pressure:** "pay today", "account will be closed", "urgent approval needed".
2. **A change of bank details** or payment instructions, especially from a supplier or customer.
3. **A sender address that is slightly wrong**, for example an extra letter, a different ending, or a free email service instead of the company domain.
4. **Unexpected attachments**, especially invoices, `.zip` files, or Office documents asking you to "enable content" or "enable macros".
5. **Links** that ask you to log in, "verify your account" or download something. Hover over a link to see where it really goes before clicking.
6. **Requests for passwords, codes or personal information.**
7. **Requests to keep something secret** or to bypass normal procedures.
8. **Unusual tone, spelling or formatting** for that sender.

## 4. Rules

1. **Do not click links or open attachments** in a message you were not expecting until you are satisfied it is genuine.
2. **Never enter your password** on a page you reached from an email link. Go to the website yourself by typing the address you know.
3. **Never share passwords or one-time codes** by email, message or phone, with anyone.
4. **Verify all payment and bank-detail changes.** Any request to change a supplier's, customer's or colleague's bank details, or to make an unusual or urgent payment, must be confirmed by **phone call to a number already held in company records**, not a number provided in the request. Changes must be approved by a manager before any payment is made to new details.
5. **Be careful with LS Electric impersonation.** Criminals may pretend to be LS Electric or its staff because they know Savikro is its distributor. Treat unexpected requests about pricing, orders, payments or account details as suspicious until verified.
6. **Do not forward** a suspicious email to colleagues "to ask what they think". Report it instead (see Section 5).
7. **Do not reply** to a suspicious message or call a phone number it provides.
8. **Auto-forwarding** work email to personal addresses is not allowed.

## 5. Reporting security incidents

### 5.1 What to report

Report anything that you think *might* be a security problem, including:

| Incident type on the Platform | Examples |
|-------------------------------|----------|
| Suspicious email / phishing | Fake invoices, bank-change requests, fake login pages, unexpected attachments. |
| Lost or stolen device | A company phone, laptop, tablet or USB drive, or a personal phone with work email. |
| Unauthorised access | Someone using your account, an unexpected lock-out email, a stranger at a terminal. |
| Data loss or exposure | An email sent to the wrong person, a price list left at a customer site, missing documents. |
| Malware or suspicious file | Pop-ups, a computer behaving strangely, a file you opened that did something unexpected. |
| Other | Anything else that worries you. |

### 5.2 How to report

1. Tap **Report an incident** on any page of the Platform.
2. Choose the type, describe what happened, and attach a screenshot or the email file if you can. You do not need to decide how serious it is; the system sets that automatically.
3. Note the reference number (for example `INC-2026-0001`) and follow progress under **My Reports**.
4. For a lost device, or if you clicked a link or entered your password on a suspicious page, **also tell the Administrator or your manager by phone straight away**, so that passwords can be reset quickly.

### 5.3 When to report

Report **immediately**, as soon as you notice. Do not wait until you are sure. An early report of something harmless costs nothing; a late report of something real can be very costly.

### 5.4 No blame for reporting

Reporting is always the right thing to do. Nobody will be disciplined for reporting a genuine concern in good faith, including their own mistake such as clicking a link. Hiding a mistake is far more harmful than making one.

## 6. Handling of reports

1. The Administrator reviews every report, sets its final severity, and moves it through *Open → In review → Resolved → Closed*, recording the actions taken.
2. High-severity reports, such as a lost device or data exposure, are sent to the Administrator immediately.
3. Reporters can see the status of their own reports but not other people's.
4. Reports are kept as a record so that recurring problems can be identified and addressed through updated policies or training.

## 7. Phishing awareness exercises

Savikro may send authorised simulated phishing emails, as described in the *Acceptable Use Policy* (POL-AUP-001). Reporting a suspicious email through the Platform is always correct, whether the email turns out to be real or simulated.

## 8. Breaches of this policy

Ignoring the payment verification procedure, sharing passwords or deliberately failing to report a known incident may result in disciplinary action.

## 9. Review

This policy is reviewed at least once a year and after any significant security incident.
