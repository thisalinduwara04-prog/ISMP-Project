# Password & Access Control Policy

**Policy code:** POL-ACP-001 · **Version:** 1.0 · **Applies to:** All staff
**Policy owner:** Administrator · **Approved by:** Savikro Enterprises Management

## 1. Purpose

Most security incidents start with someone logging in who should not. This policy makes sure that only authorised people can reach Savikro systems and information, and that each person can only reach what their job requires.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all staff and to every Savikro account, including computers, email, business software, cloud services, shared warehouse terminals and the Compliance Platform.

## 3. Principles

1. **One person, one account.** Every user has their own account. Shared or generic logins are not allowed unless the Administrator has approved them in writing for a specific purpose.
2. **Least privilege.** Access is granted according to role and department, and only to what is needed for the job.
3. **You are responsible for your account.** Anything done with your login is treated as done by you.

## 4. Password rules

1. Passwords must be at least **12 characters** long. A passphrase of several unrelated words (for example, `river-lamp-tiger-42`) is easier to remember and harder to guess than a short complex password.
2. Do not use your name, employee ID, date of birth, family names, "Savikro", "LS Electric", or common patterns such as `Password123` or `12345678`.
3. Use a **different password** for each work system, and never reuse a work password on a personal website or app.
4. Never share your password with anyone, including your manager, colleagues or someone claiming to be from IT or LS Electric. The Administrator will never ask for your password.
5. Do not write passwords on sticky notes, in notebooks left on desks, or in unprotected files. If you need to store passwords, use a password manager approved by the Administrator.
6. You must change your password:
   - on first login, when you are given a temporary password;
   - immediately, if you think someone else may know it;
   - when the Administrator asks you to.
7. Do not let browsers on shared computers save your password.

## 5. Multi-factor authentication

Where a system supports multi-factor authentication (a code from a phone app or text message in addition to the password), it must be turned on for email accounts and for any account that can access Restricted information, as defined in POL-DHC-001.

## 6. Protecting sessions

1. Lock your screen whenever you step away from a device.
2. Always log out of shared warehouse terminals and shared office computers when you finish.
3. The Platform automatically ends inactive sessions and may ask you to re-enter your password before showing sensitive information such as compliance dashboards. This is intentional.
4. After 5 incorrect login attempts, the Platform locks the account for 15 minutes and emails the account holder. If you receive a lock-out email you did not cause, report it through the Platform (incident type: *Unauthorised access*).

## 7. Access rights

| Role | Typical access |
|------|---------------|
| **Employee** | Own tasks, policies and training relevant to their role and department; own incident reports. |
| **Manager** | Everything an Employee has, plus compliance information for their own department only. |
| **Administrator** | User management, policy and training authoring, incident handling and organisation-wide reporting. |

Access outside these defaults must be requested through a manager and approved by the Administrator.

## 8. Joiners, movers and leavers

1. **Joiners:** accounts are created by the Administrator only after management confirms the new starter's role and department. Temporary passwords must be changed at first login.
2. **Movers:** when someone changes role or department, their access is updated on the day of the change and access they no longer need is removed.
3. **Leavers:** accounts are deactivated on or before the last working day, and all active sessions are ended. Shared passwords the leaver knew, such as Wi-Fi or shared mailbox passwords, must be changed.
4. Leaver accounts are deactivated, not deleted, so that records of their acknowledgements and training remain available for audit.

## 9. Access reviews

The Administrator reviews all user accounts and access rights at least every six months, and managers confirm that their staff's access is still correct. Unused or unnecessary accounts are deactivated.

## 10. Administrator accounts

1. Administrator access is limited to the smallest practical number of people.
2. Administrators must use a separate, strong password for administrative accounts and must not use them for everyday tasks such as reading email or browsing the web where a separate standard account is available.

## 11. Breaches of this policy

Sharing passwords, using someone else's account or attempting to gain access you are not authorised for may lead to access being removed and to disciplinary action.

## 12. Review

This policy is reviewed at least once a year.
