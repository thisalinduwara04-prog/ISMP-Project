## Part 1: How collection fraud works

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### The easiest way to steal stock is to ask for it

Thieves rarely break in. It is easier to walk up to the counter and collect someone else's order. Common tricks:

- **The fake collector.** Someone arrives saying "I'm from ABC Contractors, here for their breakers", using a real customer's name they found online.
- **The late change.** A call or message just before dispatch: "Please deliver to our new site instead", or "A different driver is coming today".
- **The rushed driver.** "My boss is waiting, I don't have time for paperwork."

High-value items — drives, moulded-case and air circuit breakers — are the usual targets, because they are easy to resell.

**Every one of these works only if someone skips a check.**

## Part 2: Releasing an order, step by step

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 4 min*

### Someone has arrived to collect an order. Here is what to do.

#### Step 1 — Find the order in the system
Release goods only against a valid order or delivery note. No order, no goods.

#### Step 2 — Check who they are
Ask for identification and compare it with the **authorised collector** on the order. A company name alone is not enough.

#### Step 3 — Changed details? Check with Sales first
If the collector, vehicle or delivery address has changed, do not release. Ask Sales to confirm the change with the customer by phone, using a number already in company records.

#### Step 4 — Keep the paperwork minimal
The delivery note shows only what is needed for the delivery — never prices, margins or other customers' details.

#### Step 5 — Get a signature
Get a signed proof of delivery, and book the release under **your own** login.

**If you are pressured to rush, slow down.** Genuine customers understand checks; fraudsters hate them.

## Part 3: Keeping stock records honest

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### The stock record is evidence

When stock goes missing, the system record is how we find out what happened — but only if it is accurate.

- Record every movement at the time it happens, under your own login.
- Never share your login. If a colleague uses it, their mistakes — or worse — are recorded as yours.
- Adjustments and write-offs need the Warehouse Manager's approval, with the reason recorded.
- **Never change a record to cover a shortage.** Report the difference so it can be investigated. Honest mistakes are not punished; hidden ones are.

### Question 1

A man arrives saying he is from a well-known contractor, here to collect their drives. He is not the authorised collector on the order. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Release the goods — the contractor is a regular customer
- [ ] **B.** Release them if he shows a business card
- [x] **C.** Do not release them; ask Sales to confirm with the customer using the number in company records
- [ ] **D.** Call the phone number he gives you to check

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Only the authorised collector may take the goods. A change has to be confirmed with the customer through a number we already hold — not one the visitor provides.

### Question 2

A caller asks you to change an order's delivery address just before dispatch. What is the right response?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Change it — the caller knew the order number
- [x] **B.** Ask Sales to confirm the change with the customer before dispatch
- [ ] **C.** Deliver to both addresses
- [ ] **D.** Ask the caller to email the new address and use that

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Last-minute address changes are a common fraud. Knowing an order number does not prove who someone is.

### Question 3

It is acceptable to let a colleague use your login to book out stock when you are busy.

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** Every movement is recorded against the login used. Sharing it makes the record wrong and makes you responsible for someone else's actions.

### Question 4

What should a delivery note given to a driver include? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** The items and quantities to deliver
- [x] **B.** The delivery address
- [ ] **C.** The price the customer is paying
- [ ] **D.** Other customers' orders on the same route

**Answer:** A, B  
**Why:** Drivers need only what is required to deliver. Prices and other customers' details are Confidential.

### Question 5

During a stocktake you find three breakers missing. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Adjust the record quietly so the numbers match
- [ ] **B.** Wait to see if they turn up next month
- [x] **C.** Report the difference to the Warehouse Manager so it can be investigated
- [ ] **D.** Book them out as damaged

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Changing records to hide a shortage destroys the evidence needed to find out what happened. Report it — honest reporting is never punished.
