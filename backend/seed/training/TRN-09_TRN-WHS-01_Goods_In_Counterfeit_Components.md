## Part 1: Why counterfeit components matter

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### A fake breaker can look perfect — and fail when it matters most

Circuit breakers, contactors and relays are safety devices. A genuine breaker trips when there is a fault. A counterfeit one may not — and the result can be a fire, an injury, or equipment destroyed at a customer's factory.

Counterfeit electrical parts are made to look genuine. They often enter the supply chain through:
- unexpected or unofficial deliveries;
- returns, where a customer sends back a fake in place of the genuine item;
- "cheap stock" offers from unknown sellers.

Because Savikro is the authorised LS Electric distributor, our customers trust that everything we sell is genuine. One counterfeit sale damages that trust — and could make Savikro responsible for the harm it causes.

**Your checks at goods-in are the first line of defence.**

## Part 2: Checking a delivery, step by step

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 4 min*

### A delivery has arrived. Here is how to check it.

#### Step 1 — Match the paperwork
Check the delivery against the **purchase order** and the supplier's **delivery note**: supplier, item codes, quantities. If there is no matching purchase order, do not accept it into stock.

#### Step 2 — Inspect the packaging
Compare with genuine stock you have handled before. Look for:
- poor-quality printing, blurred logos or misspellings;
- broken or re-taped seals;
- boxes that are a different size, colour or material from usual.

#### Step 3 — Inspect the product
- Labels and ratings printed clearly and matching the item code.
- Serial numbers present and matching the box — and not the same serial on many units.
- No signs of previous use, re-painting or replaced labels.

#### Step 4 — Record it under your own login
Book the goods in at the time you check them, under **your own** account.

#### Step 5 — If anything does not match
Stop. Do not put it on the shelf. Move it to quarantine (Part 3).

## Part 3: Quarantine: when in doubt, keep it out

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### What to do with goods you are not sure about

1. **Move it** to the quarantine area. Never mix it with saleable stock.
2. **Label it** clearly: "Do not issue".
3. **Photograph** the packaging, labels and serial numbers.
4. **Report it** on the Platform and tell the Warehouse Manager.
5. **Wait.** The goods stay in quarantine until the Warehouse Manager clears them. They must never be sold, used or returned to the shelf before then.

#### Returns get the same checks
A returned item must be inspected the same way before it goes back into stock. A customer returning a "faulty" breaker may — knowingly or not — be returning a counterfeit.

**You will never be in trouble for quarantining something that turns out to be genuine.**

### Question 2

Which of these are warning signs of counterfeit or tampered goods? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** Blurred logos or misspellings on the packaging
- [x] **B.** The same serial number on many units
- [x] **C.** Broken or re-taped seals
- [ ] **D.** Packaging identical to previous genuine stock

**Answer:** A, B, C  
**Why:** Poor printing, repeated serial numbers and broken seals all point to counterfeiting or tampering. Matching genuine packaging is what you expect.

### Question 3

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** A breaker is a safety device. A counterfeit may fail to trip on a fault, which can cause fire, injury or equipment damage.

A customer returns a breaker as "faulty". Before it goes back into stock, what should happen?
