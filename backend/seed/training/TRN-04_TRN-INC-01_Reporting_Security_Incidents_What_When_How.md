## Part 1: What counts as an incident?

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### If it worries you, it counts

A security incident is anything that might put Savikro's information, money, devices or accounts at risk. You do **not** need to be sure something bad has happened before you report it.

### The six incident types on the platform

| Type | Savikro examples |
|---|---|
| **Suspicious email / phishing** | A "supplier" asking to change bank details, a fake LS Electric order, a "mailbox full" login link |
| **Lost or stolen device** | A company phone left at a delivery site, a missing warehouse tablet, a stolen laptop bag |
| **Unauthorised access** | A lock-out email you didn't cause, someone using your login, a visitor at a warehouse terminal |
| **Data loss or exposure** | A quotation emailed to the wrong contractor, a price list left on a customer counter |
| **Malware or suspicious file** | Pop-ups, a computer suddenly very slow, a file that did something strange when opened |
| **Other** | Anything else that doesn't feel right |

### What is *not* an incident?

A printer jam or a forgotten password with no sign of misuse is an IT problem, not a security incident. Ask the Administrator for help with those. If you're unsure, report it anyway — the Administrator will sort it out.

## Part 2: Using the Report an incident form

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 3.5 min*

### Reporting takes less than two minutes

#### Step 1 — Open the form
Tap **Report an incident**. It is available from every page of the platform, on a computer or a phone.

#### Step 2 — Choose the type
Pick the closest of the six types. If nothing fits, choose **Other**. You don't need to decide how serious it is — the system sets an initial severity automatically, and the Administrator confirms it.

#### Step 3 — Describe what happened
Answer three simple questions in your own words:
- **What** happened? ("I clicked a link in an email about an unpaid invoice.")
- **When** did it happen? ("About 10:15 this morning.")
- **What was affected?** ("My work email. I typed my password into the page.")

#### Step 4 — Attach evidence if you can
A screenshot of the email or the page is very helpful. Don't forward the suspicious email to colleagues to get a screenshot — take it yourself.

#### Step 5 — Submit and note the reference
You'll see a reference number such as **INC-2026-0001**. Keep it.

#### Step 6 — Phone if it's urgent
For a **lost device**, or if you **entered your password** or **opened an attachment**, also phone the Administrator or your manager straight away.

#### Step 7 — Follow progress
Open **My Reports** to see your report move through **Open → In review → Resolved → Closed**. You can only see your own reports.

## Part 3: Why speed beats certainty — and why there's no blame

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Two stories

**Story A.** A salesperson emails a dealer price list to the wrong contractor. She notices five minutes later and reports it. The Administrator phones the contractor, who deletes the email. Problem solved in half an hour.

**Story B.** A warehouse tablet goes missing on a Friday. The driver hopes it will turn up and says nothing until Tuesday. By then, the stock app and email on the tablet have been open to whoever found it for four days.

The only real difference between these stories is **how quickly someone reported**.

### Savikro's no-blame promise

Our Email Security & Incident Reporting Policy (POL-EML-001) is clear:

- Nobody will be disciplined for reporting a genuine concern in good faith — **including their own mistake**.
- An early report of something harmless costs nothing.
- **Hiding** a mistake is far more harmful than making one.

### Good reporting habits
- Report as soon as you notice — don't wait until you're sure.
- Don't try to investigate or "fix" it yourself.
- Don't delete evidence such as the suspicious email.
- Tell the truth about what you did; it helps the Administrator act quickly.
