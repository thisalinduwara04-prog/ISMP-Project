## Part 1: Shared terminals and your account

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Your login is your signature

Anything done with your account is recorded as done by **you** — every stock change, every delivery confirmation, every policy acknowledgement.

#### On a shared warehouse terminal
- **Lock** the screen every time you walk away (Windows key + L).
- **Log out** fully at the end of your shift.
- Never let the browser **save your password**.
- Never let a colleague "just use your login for a minute". Everyone has their own account.

#### Passwords
When you change your password, the platform shows a checklist. Your new password must have **all five**:
- at least **8 characters**
- an **uppercase** letter (A–Z)
- a **lowercase** letter (a–z)
- a **number** (0–9)
- a **special character**, such as ! @ # $ % & * ?

The **Update password** button only becomes active when every item is ticked.

Ticking the boxes isn't enough on its own. *Savikro@2026* passes the checklist but is easy to guess. Join a few unrelated words with a number and a symbol instead, e.g. *Tiger!Lamp-River7* (don't use this exact example).
- Don't use your name, employee ID, birthday, "Savikro" or "LS Electric".
- Don't write it on a sticky note on the terminal.
- If you think someone knows it, change it straight away. Changing your password **signs you out on every other device**, so anyone else using your account is logged out.

#### Lock-out emails
After 5 wrong attempts your account locks for 15 minutes and you get an email. If you get one and it **wasn't you**, someone may be trying your account — report it as **Unauthorised access**.

## Part 2: Lost phone or tablet: step by step

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 3 min*

### You can't find a company phone or tablet. Here's what to do.

#### Step 1 — Check quickly, but don't wait long
Check the vehicle, the last delivery site and the warehouse. If it's not found within about 30 minutes, treat it as lost.

#### Step 2 — Report it on the platform
From another device, open **Report an incident → Lost or stolen device**. Say what device it was, when and where you last had it, and what was on it (email, stock app, photos of delivery notes). The system marks it **High** severity automatically.

#### Step 3 — Phone the Administrator or your manager
A lost device is urgent. A call lets them lock or wipe the device and reset passwords right away.

#### Step 4 — Change your passwords
If your work email or apps were signed in on the device, change those passwords from another device.

#### Step 5 — Help with follow-up
Check **My Reports** for updates, and let the Administrator know if the device turns up.

**Remember:** a lost device reported in 10 minutes is a small problem. The same device reported three days later can be a big one. You won't be in trouble for reporting.

## Part 3: Stock records, deliveries and visitors

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Stock information is Confidential

Stock levels, incoming shipments and delivery schedules tell a competitor — or a thief — exactly what we have and when. Share them only with people who need them for the job.

#### Deliveries and collections
- Check the identity of anyone collecting goods against the order **before** releasing stock.
- Give drivers only the delivery details they need.
- Don't leave delivery notes, invoices or pick lists on dashboards or at customer counters.

#### Visitors in the warehouse
- Don't let visitors, drivers or contractors use warehouse terminals.
- Keep screens showing stock or customer data turned away from visitors.
- Ask unfamiliar people who they are here to see, and inform your supervisor.

#### USB drives and chargers
- Never plug unknown USB drives into warehouse terminals — even ones "found" in the car park.
- Use only company-provided cables and chargers on company devices.

### Question 1

You need to step away from the warehouse terminal for two minutes. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Leave it — it's only two minutes
- [x] **B.** Lock the screen (Windows key + L)
- [ ] **C.** Ask a colleague to keep working on your login
- [ ] **D.** Turn off the monitor only

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Anything done on your unlocked session is recorded as you. Locking takes one second.

### Question 2

It is acceptable to let a trusted colleague use your login if theirs isn't working.

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** One person, one account. Sharing logins breaks the audit trail and the Password & Access Control Policy. Ask the Administrator to fix their account.

### Question 3

Which password is the strongest?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** savikro123
- [ ] **B.** Savikro@2026
- [x] **C.** Tiger!Lamp-River7
- [ ] **D.** Pa55word

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Only Tiger!Lamp-River7 meets all five requirements (8+ characters, uppercase, lowercase, number, special character) without being easy to guess. Savikro@2026 passes the checklist but uses the company name and year. The other two miss requirements.

### Question 4

You can't find the company tablet after a delivery. After a quick search, what should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Wait a few days in case it turns up
- [x] **B.** Report it as 'Lost or stolen device' and phone the Administrator
- [ ] **C.** Buy a replacement quietly
- [ ] **D.** Only tell a colleague

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** A lost device is high severity. Reporting and calling quickly allows the device to be locked and passwords reset before harm is done.

### Question 5

You find a USB drive in the car park labelled 'Salary 2026'. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Plug it into the warehouse terminal to find the owner
- [x] **B.** Hand it to the Administrator without plugging it in
- [ ] **C.** Plug it into your personal laptop instead
- [ ] **D.** Keep it for your own use

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Dropped USB drives with tempting labels are a known way to spread malware. Never plug in unknown devices.

### Question 6

Which of these should you do before releasing goods to someone collecting an order? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** Check their identity against the order
- [x] **B.** Confirm the order details match
- [ ] **C.** Let them use the terminal to look up their own order
- [ ] **D.** Show them current stock levels so they can order more

**Answer:** A, B  
**Why:** Verify the collector and the order. Visitors should never use terminals, and stock levels are Confidential.

### Question 7

You receive an email saying your account was locked after 5 failed logins, but you haven't tried to log in. What does this suggest?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Nothing — ignore it
- [x] **B.** Someone may be trying to get into your account — report it as 'Unauthorised access'
- [ ] **C.** The system is broken
- [ ] **D.** You should reply to the email with your password

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** An unexpected lock-out can mean someone is guessing your password. Report it and consider changing your password.

### Question 8

Stock levels and delivery schedules can be shared freely with drivers and visitors because they are not financial data.

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** Stock and delivery information is Confidential — it tells competitors and thieves what we hold and when.
