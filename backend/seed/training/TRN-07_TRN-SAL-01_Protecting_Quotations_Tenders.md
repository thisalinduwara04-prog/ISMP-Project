## Part 1: Why our pricing is worth stealing

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### Our prices are a competitor's shopping list

Electrical contractors and panel builders often ask several distributors to quote for the same job — the same breakers, contactors and drives. If a competitor learns what we quoted, they can undercut us by a small margin and win the order.

Pricing information leaks in ordinary ways:
- a quotation sent to the wrong "John" because email auto-complete picked the wrong contact;
- one customer's quotation edited into another's, with the first customer's prices still hidden in an old line;
- a price list forwarded from a personal WhatsApp to a group;
- a discount discussed out loud at a busy trade counter.

#### What is Confidential and what is Restricted
- **Confidential:** customer quotations, dealer and contractor price lists, customer account details.
- **Restricted:** LS Electric supplier pricing, our cost prices and margins.

Never reveal cost prices, margins or another customer's discount — not in writing, not in conversation, not on your screen during a visit.

## Part 2: Sending a quotation safely

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 4 min*

### A contractor asks you for a quote. Here is how to send it safely.

#### Step 1 — Start from a clean template
Create a new quotation from the template. Never copy and edit another customer's quotation: old prices, names and discounts can survive in hidden rows or notes.

#### Step 2 — Check who it is for
Confirm the customer's name and their email address **from company records**. Read the address letter by letter before you press Send.

#### Step 3 — Watch for a changed address
If the customer asks you to send it to a new email address — for example a Gmail address "because our server is down" — call them on the number in company records to confirm before sending.

#### Step 4 — Send it as a PDF
Send a PDF, not an editable spreadsheet. A spreadsheet can expose formulas, cost columns or hidden sheets.

#### Step 5 — Discounts in writing
If the price needs a discount beyond your limit, get the Sales Manager's approval through company email or systems — not a voice note or personal chat.

**Remember:** one quotation, one customer, one verified address.

## Part 3: Tenders and project bids

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### A live bid is Restricted until it is submitted

When Savikro bids for a hospital, factory or government project, the bid price is the most sensitive number we have.

#### Inside the bid team
- Only the named bid team may see the pricing.
- Keep bid files in the approved shared folder, not on your desktop or a USB drive.
- Do not print bid pricing unless you need to, and shred drafts.

#### Outside the bid team
- Never discuss a live bid with another bidder, a competitor, or a friend in the trade — including at trade events and supplier meetings.
- Drawings, panel schedules and specifications from a contractor belong to that contractor. Use them only for their project.

#### When it is over
If the bid is lost, or the customer asks for their documents back, delete or return them.

### Question 1

A contractor asks for a quotation. What is the safest way to prepare it?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Copy last week's quotation for a similar customer and change the name
- [x] **B.** Start a new quotation from the clean template
- [ ] **C.** Send the full dealer price list so they can work it out themselves
- [ ] **D.** Send an editable spreadsheet so they can adjust quantities

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Editing another customer's quotation can leak their prices and discounts. A clean template carries nothing from anyone else.

### Question 2

A known customer emails from a new Gmail address asking you to send their quotation there "because our office email is down". What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Send it — you recognise the customer's name
- [ ] **B.** Send it to both addresses to be safe
- [x] **C.** Call the customer on the number in company records to confirm before sending
- [ ] **D.** Reply to the Gmail address asking whether it is genuine

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** A new address in an unexpected message is a classic way to steal quotations. Confirm through a number you already hold — replying to the suspicious address proves nothing.

### Question 3

It is acceptable to tell a customer what discount another contractor received, as long as you do not name the contractor.

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** Other customers' discounts are Confidential whether or not a name is attached. Discussing them also tells competitors how we price.

### Question 4

Which of these are Restricted information? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** LS Electric supplier pricing
- [x] **B.** Savikro's margins on a product line
- [ ] **C.** A published LS Electric product brochure
- [ ] **D.** Our showroom opening hours

**Answer:** A, B  
**Why:** Supplier pricing and margins are Restricted. Brochures and opening hours are Public.

### Question 5

At a trade event, a friend who works for another distributor asks what price Savikro bid on a hospital project that has not closed yet. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Give a rough figure — it is only an estimate
- [ ] **B.** Tell them, since the project is public knowledge
- [x] **C.** Politely decline — a live bid is Restricted until it is submitted
- [ ] **D.** Tell them after checking nobody else is listening

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Bid pricing is Restricted until submission and must never be discussed outside the named bid team, even informally.
