## Part 1: Using company devices and systems

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Company systems are for company work

Limited personal use is fine as long as it doesn't get in the way of your work, cost the company money, or break any other rule.

### Five everyday rules

1. **Only open what you need.** Being able to open a file doesn't mean you're allowed to. Only access information you need for your job.
2. **Don't install things yourself.** Software, apps and browser extensions need the Administrator's approval first. Free tools and "helpful" extensions are a common way malware gets in.
3. **No unknown USB drives or chargers.** Don't plug in anything you don't recognise — including drives found lying around.
4. **Keep company files in company places.** Don't upload price lists, quotations or customer details to personal cloud storage, personal email or AI chat tools unless the Administrator has approved it.
5. **Don't switch off security.** Never disable antivirus, share accounts or use someone else's login.

### Lock it
Lock your screen whenever you walk away — **Windows key + L** on a computer, or the lock button on a phone or tablet. Company devices must have a PIN, password, fingerprint or face lock.

## Part 2: Out of the office: vehicles, customer sites and social media

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Much of Savikro's work happens on the road

Sales visits to contractors and panel builders, deliveries to shops and sites, meetings with dealers — our information travels with us.

### On the move
- Don't leave laptops, tablets or phones **unattended** in vehicles, at customer sites or in public places.
- Keep quotations and price lists out of sight — not on seats or dashboards.
- Before showing a customer your screen, close other customers' files.

### In conversation
- Don't discuss pricing, margins or other customers' deals in public places, shops or at customer sites where others can hear.

### On social media
- Don't post about confidential business matters such as pricing, margins, upcoming LS Electric stock or customer deals.
- Be careful with photos: a picture of the warehouse or your desk can show stock levels, delivery notes or a screen full of customer data.

### Leaving Savikro
Return all company devices, keys and access cards on or before your last day.

## Part 3: Your tasks, phishing exercises and what the platform records

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Your compliance tasks

- **My Tasks** shows the policies and training assigned to you, with due dates.
- **Acknowledging** a policy means you confirm you have read it, understood it, and will follow it. Only acknowledge once you've actually read it.
- Your platform account is personal. Never share your password or let someone use your session.

### Phishing awareness exercises

With written management approval, Savikro may send **simulated phishing emails** to help everyone practise.

- Exercises are not announced in advance — that's what makes them useful.
- If you click, you'll see a page explaining it was an exercise and showing the warning signs. **No passwords or data are collected.**
- Individual results are seen **only by the Administrator**. Managers see department totals only.
- Results are **not** used for performance reviews or discipline.
- Reporting the email is always the right response, real or simulated.

### What the platform records

The platform records logins, policy acknowledgements, quiz results and incident reports. This record is used to **show compliance** and to **investigate security events**. It is not used to monitor your general work performance.

### Question 1

You find a free browser extension that would make building quotations faster. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Install it — it helps you work
- [x] **B.** Ask the Administrator to approve it before installing
- [ ] **C.** Install it on a shared computer instead
- [ ] **D.** Ask a colleague to install it for you

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Software, apps and extensions need the Administrator's approval. Unapproved tools are a common route for malware.
