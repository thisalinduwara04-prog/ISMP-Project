## Part 1: Two people for every payment

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### Dual control stops both fraud and honest mistakes

Every payment — including remittances to LS Electric — follows the same rule: **one person prepares it, a different authorised person approves it.** Nobody approves a payment they prepared.

Dual control catches:
- a fake invoice that looks like a real supplier's;
- a duplicate invoice that has already been paid;
- a typing mistake in an amount;
- pressure to pay "urgently" outside the normal process.

#### Before approving, the approver checks
- The supplier and bank details match **company records**, not just the invoice.
- The invoice has not been paid before.
- The goods or services were actually received.

An urgent request that claims to come from a director is checked by phone using a number in company records — however senior the name on it.

## Part 2: Keeping records safe and recoverable

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### A file only on your desktop is a file waiting to be lost

Computers fail, get stolen, and get hit by ransomware. Records that exist in only one place can be lost for good.

- Save invoices, ledgers and payroll files in the **approved shared location**, which the Administrator backs up. Not on your desktop, a USB drive or personal cloud storage.
- Keep cheque books, the company stamp and signed blank documents locked away.
- Keep financial and payroll records for the period required by Sri Lankan tax and company law, as advised by Savikro's accountant.
- If you see a ransom message, files you cannot open, or files with strange new names, **unplug the network cable, do not switch off, and report it immediately**.

## Part 3: Disposing of records and old devices

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 4 min*

### A batch of old invoices, and an old office PC, are no longer needed. Here is what to do.

#### Step 1 — Check the retention period
Confirm with your manager that the records are past the period the law requires. If in doubt, keep them.

#### Step 2 — Shred paper
Shred printed invoices, payroll sheets and bank statements. Never put them in general waste or reuse them as scrap paper.

#### Step 3 — Hand devices to the Administrator
Old computers, laptops, phones, USB drives and printer hard disks go to the Administrator for **secure wiping**. Deleting files or emptying the Recycle Bin is not enough — the data can be recovered.

#### Step 4 — Record what was destroyed
Note what was disposed of, when, and by whom.

#### Step 5 — Never sell or gift an unwiped device
A second-hand office PC can carry years of customer and staff data out of the building.

### Question 1

You prepared a supplier payment. Who should approve it?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** You, once you have double-checked it
- [x] **B.** A different authorised person
- [ ] **C.** The supplier, by email
- [ ] **D.** Nobody, if it is under the usual amount

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Dual control means the person who prepares a payment never approves it. A second person catches fraud and mistakes.

### Question 2

Before approving a payment, what should the approver check? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** The bank details match company records
- [x] **B.** The invoice has not already been paid
- [x] **C.** The goods or services were actually received
- [ ] **D.** The invoice logo looks professional

**Answer:** A, B, C  
**Why:** Fake invoices often look professional. Matching bank details, checking for duplicates and confirming delivery are what catch them.

### Question 3

Deleting files and emptying the Recycle Bin is enough before giving away an old office computer.

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** Deleted files can be recovered. Old devices must be securely wiped by the Administrator before disposal or reuse.

### Question 4

Where should you save this month's invoices?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** On your desktop, for quick access
- [ ] **B.** On a USB drive you keep in your bag
- [x] **C.** In the approved shared location that the Administrator backs up
- [ ] **D.** In your personal cloud storage

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Only the approved shared location is backed up. Files elsewhere can be lost to a hardware failure, theft or ransomware.

### Question 5

Your screen shows a message demanding payment to unlock your files. What should you do first?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Pay the ransom to recover the files quickly
- [ ] **B.** Switch the computer off and go home
- [x] **C.** Unplug the network cable, leave the computer on, and report it immediately
- [ ] **D.** Try to open the files again

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Disconnecting the network stops ransomware spreading to shared files. Leaving the computer on preserves evidence, and fast reporting limits the damage.
