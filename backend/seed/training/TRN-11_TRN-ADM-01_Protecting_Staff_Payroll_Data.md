## Part 1: Personal data and the law

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### Staff records are Restricted

The Administration team holds the most personal information in the company: national identity numbers, addresses, bank accounts, salaries, EPF/ETF records, medical certificates and disciplinary notes.

In Sri Lanka, the **Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022** sets rules for handling personal data. In practice it means:
- **Collect only what is needed** for employment and payroll.
- **Use it only for that purpose** — not, for example, to look up a colleague's salary out of curiosity.
- **Keep it accurate**, and update it when staff tell you of a change.
- **Protect it**, so only the people who need it can see it.

Only named HR and payroll staff may open staff files. Being in the Administration department does not by itself give you access.

## Part 2: Answering a request for staff information

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 4 min*

### Someone asks you for information about a member of staff. Here is what to do.

#### Step 1 — Who is asking?
Requests come from many places: a bank checking a loan, a caller claiming to be a relative, a manager, the staff member themselves. Never assume.

#### Step 2 — Confirm their identity
For a staff member asking about their own record, confirm it is them — for example, by calling back on the number in their HR file. For anyone else, find out exactly who they are and why they need it.

#### Step 3 — Check they are entitled to it
- A manager may need someone's job title and start date — but not their salary or medical details.
- A bank or landlord asking for salary confirmation needs the **employee's written consent**.
- A "relative" on the phone is not entitled to anything.

#### Step 4 — Share only what is needed, through a safe channel
Send it to a verified address, never to a new email address given during the call.

#### Step 5 — If it feels wrong, stop
Pressure, urgency, or someone "from head office" you do not recognise are warning signs. Decline politely and tell your manager.

## Part 3: Payslips, files and the clear desk

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### Everyday habits that protect colleagues

- **Payslips** go only to the employee's own registered email address or in a sealed envelope addressed to them.
- **Paper HR files** live in the locked cabinet. Lock it whenever you leave the room.
- **Electronic files** stay in the restricted shared folder — never on your desktop, a USB drive or personal email.
- **Printing:** collect payroll printouts immediately. Shred drafts and spoiled copies.
- **Clear desk:** at the end of the day, nothing with staff data is left on desks or printers.
- **Screens:** lock your computer whenever you step away, and keep payroll screens turned away from visitors.

### Question 1

A caller says she is a staff member's wife and asks for his salary to complete a loan form. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Give it — she knows his employee number
- [x] **B.** Decline politely; salary details are shared only with the employee's written consent
- [ ] **C.** Give an approximate figure
- [ ] **D.** Email it to the address she provides

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** A relative is not entitled to staff personal data. Salary confirmation for a loan needs the employee's own written consent.

### Question 2

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** Staff records are Restricted. Only named HR and payroll staff may access them, and only for their job.

### Question 3

Which are safe ways to deliver a payslip? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** To the employee's own registered email address
- [x] **B.** In a sealed envelope addressed to the employee
- [ ] **C.** Posted in the staff WhatsApp group
- [ ] **D.** Left on the employee's desk, unsealed

**Answer:** A, B  
**Why:** A payslip must reach only the employee. Group chats and open desks expose salary details to others.

### Question 4

A department manager asks for a list of their team's salaries "to plan the budget". What is the best response?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Send the full payroll export
- [x] **B.** Check with the Administration Manager what the manager is entitled to see before sharing anything
- [ ] **C.** Send it, since they are a manager
- [ ] **D.** Read the figures out over the phone

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Being a manager does not automatically entitle someone to salary data. Share only what has been approved, and only what is needed.

### Question 5

Under the Personal Data Protection Act, which principle means you should not look up a colleague's salary out of curiosity?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Keep data accurate
- [x] **B.** Use personal data only for the purpose it was collected for
- [ ] **C.** Collect as much data as possible
- [ ] **D.** Store data on a USB drive

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Payroll data is collected to pay staff, not for anyone's curiosity. Using it for any other purpose breaks the purpose-limitation principle.
