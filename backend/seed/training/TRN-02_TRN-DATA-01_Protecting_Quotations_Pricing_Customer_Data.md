## Part 1: The four information levels

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Not all information is equal

Savikro uses four levels. When unsure, treat it as **Confidential** and ask.

| Level | Think of it as… | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Anyone may see it | Brochures, public datasheets, advertised retail prices |
| **Internal** | Staff only | Staff notices, delivery schedules |
| **Confidential** | Only people who need it | Quotations, dealer price lists, customer contact details, stock levels, invoices |
| **Restricted** | Named people only | LS Electric supplier/cost pricing, margins, distributor agreements, customer contracts, staff salary data |

#### A quick test
Ask yourself: *"If a competitor or another customer saw this, would it hurt us?"*
- No → Public or Internal
- Yes → Confidential
- Yes, badly → Restricted

## Part 2: Sending a quotation safely

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 3.5 min*

### Follow these steps every time you send a quotation

#### Step 1 — Start from a clean template
Never open an old quotation for Customer A and edit it for Customer B. Hidden rows, old discounts or the previous customer's name are easy to miss.

#### Step 2 — Check the content
Make sure the quotation shows only the selling prices for **this** customer. Cost prices, supplier prices and margins must never appear.

#### Step 3 — Check the recipient
Type the address carefully or choose it from the customer's record. Check it letter by letter — auto-complete often picks the wrong contact.

#### Step 4 — Use BCC for group emails
When sending a notice to several dealers, put their addresses in **BCC** so they cannot see each other's details.

#### Step 5 — Use company channels only
Send from your company email, not personal WhatsApp or personal email.

#### Step 6 — If it goes wrong
If you send a quotation to the wrong person, report it immediately: **Report an incident → Data loss or exposure**. The sooner we know, the sooner we can ask the recipient to delete it.

## Part 3: Customer visits, paper and disposal

*Type: ARTICLE · About 2.5 min*

### Out and about

- Don't discuss pricing, margins or other customers' discounts at customer sites, in shops or on the phone in public.
- Don't leave price lists or quotations on vehicle seats or dashboards.
- Lock your laptop or tablet screen before turning it towards a customer, and close other files first.
- If a customer asks for "the prices you gave another contractor", politely decline.

### In the office

- Collect printouts immediately.
- Lock Confidential and Restricted papers away at the end of the day (clear-desk rule).
- Keep work files in approved company locations — not personal cloud storage, personal email or USB drives.

### Disposal

- **Shred** Confidential and Restricted paper. Never use it as scrap.
- Give old phones, laptops and storage devices to the Administrator for secure wiping. Deleting files is not enough.

### Question 1

What classification level applies to LS Electric supplier cost prices?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Public
- [ ] **B.** Internal
- [ ] **C.** Confidential
- [x] **D.** Restricted

**Answer:** D  
**Why:** Supplier and cost pricing could seriously harm Savikro and its relationship with LS Electric if leaked, so it is Restricted.

### Question 2

You need to send a quotation to Customer B. What is the safest way to prepare it?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Edit Customer A's quotation and change the name
- [x] **B.** Start from a clean quotation template
- [ ] **C.** Copy the full price list into the email
- [ ] **D.** Ask Customer A to forward theirs

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Editing another customer's quotation risks leaving their details, discounts or hidden rows in the document.

### Question 3

You are emailing a price update to 25 dealers at once. Where should their addresses go?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** To
- [ ] **B.** CC
- [x] **C.** BCC
- [ ] **D.** In the body of the email

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** BCC hides recipients from each other, so dealers cannot see who else Savikro supplies.

### Question 5

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Reporting quickly lets the Administrator contain the damage, for example by asking the recipient to delete it. Honest mistakes reported promptly are not punished.

### Question 8

You're unsure whether a document is Internal or Confidential. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Treat it as Internal to save time
- [x] **B.** Treat it as Confidential and ask your manager

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** When in doubt, choose the higher level. Over-protecting costs little; under-protecting can be costly.
