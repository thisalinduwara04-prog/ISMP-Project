## Part 1: Joiners, movers and leavers

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### Access should match the job — today

Most access problems are not hacks. They are people who still have access they no longer need.

#### Joiners
Confirm the new starter's **role and department** before the Administrator creates the account. Their department decides which policies and training are assigned to them.

#### Movers
When someone changes role or department, tell the Administrator **on the day**. Their access to the old team's information should be removed, not added to.

#### Leavers
Tell the Administrator before the last working day so the account is deactivated and every session ended. Shared passwords the person knew — Wi-Fi, shared mailboxes — must be changed. Accounts are deactivated, not deleted, so their acknowledgement and training records stay available for audit.

#### Every six months
Confirm at the access review that each of your staff's access is still correct.

## Part 2: When an incident is reported

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 4 min*

### A serious incident has been reported. Here is management's part.

#### Step 1 — Take it seriously, without blame
Thank the person who reported it. Incidents reported quickly are small; incidents hidden out of fear become large.

#### Step 2 — Understand the impact
With the Administrator, establish what happened, what information or stock is affected, and whether it is still happening.

#### Step 3 — Decide who needs to be told
If customers' or staff members' personal data may have been exposed, decide whether they — and the authorities — must be informed under the Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022. Consider whether LS Electric needs to know.

#### Step 4 — Record decisions in the Platform
Record what was decided and done in the incident's history, so there is a clear account later.

#### Step 5 — Learn from it
Ask what would stop it happening again: a policy change, a new check, or refresher training for a team.

## Part 3: Keeping the organisation compliant

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### What gets measured gets done

The Platform shows which staff have acknowledged policies and completed training — and who is overdue.

- Review the **Compliance dashboard at least monthly**.
- Follow up with departments that are falling behind. Managers can send reminders to their own staff.
- Complete your **own** acknowledgements and training on time — staff notice when management does not.
- Review security policies at least once a year, and sooner when the business or the threats change.

A high compliance figure is not the goal in itself. The goal is staff who know what to do when something goes wrong.

### Question 1

A sales representative moves to the warehouse. When should the Administrator be told?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** At the next six-monthly access review
- [x] **B.** On the day of the move
- [ ] **C.** Only if they ask for warehouse access
- [ ] **D.** It is not necessary — they keep their sales access

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Access must match the current job. The Administrator updates it on the day of the change and removes access that is no longer needed.

### Question 2

When a member of staff leaves, their account should be deleted so that nobody can use it.

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** Leaver accounts are deactivated, not deleted, so their acknowledgement and training records remain available for audit.

### Question 3

An employee reports that they emailed a customer list to the wrong address. What should management's first response be?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Start a disciplinary process immediately
- [x] **B.** Thank them for reporting, then work with the Administrator to assess the impact
- [ ] **C.** Ask them to keep it quiet
- [ ] **D.** Wait to see whether the recipient complains

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Incidents are handled without blame so that people keep reporting. The next step is understanding the impact.

### Question 4

Customer personal data may have been exposed in an incident. Which should management consider? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** Whether affected customers must be informed
- [x] **B.** Whether the authorities must be informed under the Personal Data Protection Act
- [x] **C.** Recording the decisions in the incident's history
- [ ] **D.** Deleting the incident report to limit the damage

**Answer:** A, B, C  
**Why:** Management decides who must be told and records it. Deleting records would destroy the evidence and is never acceptable.

### Question 5

How often should management review the Compliance dashboard, at minimum?

- [ ] **A.** Once a year
- [ ] **B.** Only when an audit is due
- [x] **C.** At least monthly

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Automatic reminders help, but management reviews the dashboard at least monthly and follows up with departments that are behind.
