## Part 1: Your phone and laptop on the road

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### Your phone is a filing cabinet of customer data

A sales phone holds customer contacts, price lists, quotations and email. If it is lost unlocked, all of that goes with it.

#### Lock it down
- Use a PIN or password, and set the screen to lock automatically.
- Keep company data in company apps and email — not in personal WhatsApp chats, personal email or a personal cloud account.

#### Wi-Fi
- Do not use café, hotel or customer Wi-Fi for company systems. Use your phone's mobile data or hotspot.

#### In the vehicle
- Never leave a laptop, tablet or bag visible on a seat. Take it with you, or lock it out of sight before you arrive.
- Do not leave quotations or price lists on the dashboard.

#### If it is lost
Report it on the Platform as **Lost or stolen device** straight away, then phone the Administrator so the device can be locked and your passwords reset. Ten minutes matters.

## Part 2: Spotting a suspicious order

*Type: WALKTHROUGH · About 4 min*

### Some "orders" are really attempts to steal stock. Here is how to check.

#### Step 1 — Notice the warning signs
Be alert when an order has several of these:
- an urgent, large order of high-value items such as breakers or drives;
- delivery to a new address, or a "site" you have never heard of;
- a new person you have never dealt with authorising it;
- pressure to skip the normal credit or account checks;
- an email address that is slightly different from the customer's usual one.

#### Step 2 — Do not reply to the request to check it
Replying to a suspicious email only reaches the fraudster.

#### Step 3 — Call the customer
Phone the customer on the number **in company records** and confirm the order, the delivery address and who is authorised.

#### Step 4 — Tell the warehouse
If the delivery address or collector changes, tell the warehouse only after you have confirmed it with the customer.

#### Step 5 — Report it
If anything does not check out, report it on the Platform and tell the Sales Manager. A delayed genuine order is a small problem; a stolen consignment is a large one.

## Part 3: At the customer's site

*Type: ARTICLE · About 3 min*

### Visiting a site or panel shop

- Keep your laptop or tablet screen turned away from others, especially when price lists or other customers' quotations are open.
- Do not leave printed quotations on site counters or in shared site offices.
- Ask before photographing a customer's site, drawings or equipment, and store any photos only in company systems.
- If someone at the site asks about other customers' projects or prices, politely decline.
- Do not plug a customer's USB drive into a company laptop to "copy the drawings" — ask them to email the files instead.

### Question 1

You are working at a café between visits. How should you connect to company email?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Use the café's free Wi-Fi
- [x] **B.** Use your phone's mobile data or hotspot
- [ ] **C.** Ask the customer next door for their Wi-Fi password
- [ ] **D.** Wait until you find any open network

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Public and customer Wi-Fi can be monitored. Your own mobile data is the safe option.

### Question 2

Which of these are warning signs that an order may be fraudulent? (Select all that apply)

*Multiple choice — select all that apply*

- [x] **A.** An urgent order for high-value breakers to a new delivery address
- [x] **B.** Pressure to skip the normal account checks
- [x] **C.** An email address slightly different from the customer's usual one
- [ ] **D.** A regular customer re-ordering their usual items to their usual site

**Answer:** A, B, C  
**Why:** Urgency, new addresses, skipped checks and look-alike email addresses are all signs of order fraud. A routine repeat order is normal.

### Question 3

To check whether a suspicious order is genuine, it is fine to reply to the email and ask.

*True / False*

- [ ] **True**
- [x] **False**

**Answer:** False  
**Why:** A reply goes straight back to whoever sent it — possibly the fraudster. Call the customer on the number in company records instead.

### Question 4

You realise your sales phone is missing after a site visit. What should you do first?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Wait until tomorrow in case it turns up
- [ ] **B.** Buy a new phone and restore your contacts
- [x] **C.** Report it as 'Lost or stolen device' and phone the Administrator
- [ ] **D.** Only tell your colleagues

**Answer:** C  
**Why:** Reporting quickly lets the Administrator lock the phone and reset passwords before customer data is exposed.

### Question 5

At a panel shop, the owner hands you a USB drive with drawings to copy onto your company laptop. What should you do?

*Single choice*

- [ ] **A.** Plug it in — the customer is trusted
- [x] **B.** Ask them to email the drawings instead
- [ ] **C.** Copy it onto your personal laptop instead
- [ ] **D.** Plug it in but only open the PDF files

**Answer:** B  
**Why:** Unknown USB drives can carry malware even from well-meaning customers. Email is the safe way to receive files.
